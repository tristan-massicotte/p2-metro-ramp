1. A doubling every ~5 months

×365 in about 40 months: ~16% more every month, for three years.
×365from ~2,700 a day today to 1,000,000 a day
~16% / mogrowth needed every month from Aug 2026 to Dec 2029
~4.7 modoubling time for drones, docks, chargers and spares

If one drone did 25 deliveries a day with 20% extra for charging and maintenance (both my assumptions), the target would need about 48,000 drones in service, and each month near the end would add more drones than the whole fleet of two years before.

2. What final assembly and test need

Stations grow ~6× a year, and first-pass yield is a capacity lever.
~6× / yrgrowth in drones to build each week, and in the stations to build them
1 → 20final assembly stations from 2026 to Dec 2029, with my station times
~9 h / wkof end-of-line test time per point of first-pass yield, at Dec 2029 volume

Station counts use my guesses: 45 min of final assembly and 30 min of end-of-line test per drone, 90% first-pass yield (failures retested once), two 8-hour shifts, 85% uptime, 2% of the fleet replaced each month. The counts move with those numbers; the ~6× a year doesn't, because it comes from the growth curve. So a capacity model built on time studies needs refreshing every few months, not every year.

3. Where the ramp is decided

Suppliers, contract manufacturers, final assembly and test, metro readiness.

The hardware flow as Zipline's postings describe it. Highlighted steps are my hypotheses.

  1. Watch
    Suppliers
    Capacity has to be booked for volumes that do not exist yet: two doublings ahead is ~4× today.
  2. Contract manufacturers
    Top-level assemblies built at CMs for mass production. Their capacity has to follow the same curve.
  3. Watch
    Final assembly and end-of-line test
    In-house, with a capacity model kept by time studies. At ~16% a month, the model is out of date within a quarter unless it is updated with the curve.
  4. Watch
    Metro hardware readiness
    Docks, drones, chargers and spares in place before launch day. The item with the longest lead time sets the latest date to commit (Dallas and Houston for Uber before the end of 2026, then dozens of cities).
  5. Launch and operations

My 16 weeks

  1. Wk 1–2Learn the line. Shifts in final assembly and end-of-line test, station by station, with the people who run them.
  2. Wk 3–6Refresh the capacity model. Time studies on the assemblies the team picks, and first-pass yield by test step, so the model runs on real station times and real retest load.
  3. Wk 7–11Test capacity against the curve. Compare in-house and CM capacity with the next two doublings, and list where stations, fixtures or people run out first, with Quality and Global Supply.
  4. Wk 12–16Run it and prove it. Help land one fix on the line (a station, a fixture or a test step), measure before and after, and leave the model ready to refresh every month.
